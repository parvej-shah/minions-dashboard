01 · Idea harvester

Ykpl0pQTzBdZlDcA · 43 nodes. Brand context comes from /api/brand/context, which supplies positioning, directives, author standing, tenant RSS sources, the pillar rotation for this run, and fingerprints of the 60 most recent theses, and the author context: the ICP, professional memory, content lanes, and the harvest_plan. The plan (src/lib/harvest/plan.ts) picks what each idea is built on so the tenant's source mix holds: 35% current work, 25% experience, 20% ICP questions, 15% market, 5% experiments. If that context is incomplete (context_complete: false) or has no pillar_rotation or harvest_plan, the run fails instead of falling back to generic sources.

  1. Resolve Client ID: takes client_id and the run id from the webhook body. It starts only from the webhook (dispatcher, dashboard, or 02 when no eligible idea is available) or a manual run.
  2. Read Ideas: loads up to 2,000 of the tenant's ideas.
  3. Backlog Guard: when 10 unused, gate-passed REFINED ideas are available, the run skips and reports skipped: true in the callback.
  4. Fetch Brand Context: branches into two parallel paths, A and B.
  5. A · Refine: any NEW rows are sent to the refiner (prompt below), gated in refine mode (a person's note is never dropped, only marked below the bar), and saved as REFINED with source MANUAL.
  6. B · Expand Tenant Sources → RSS: reads one feed per active source. Without sources it falls back to Hacker News, TechCrunch AI and the n8n blog. Feed health goes to /api/internal/source-status.
  7. Build Harvest Payload: groups signals by source, takes a rotating window of each source's max_items (offset by harvest_run_index), and caps the total at 18.
  8. Qwen Harvest: generates exactly one idea per plan slot. Each idea cites the memory or proof it rests on and quotes it. Signals and memory are fenced as data.
  9. Attach Source URLs → Gate Harvest Ideas → Apply Harvest Gate: maps signal_index back to the article, then /api/internal/harvest/gate (src/lib/harvest/gate.ts) decides each idea in code. PASS: an ICP problem and a quoted, used memory item or proof. BELOW_BAR: sound but resting on no experience. REJECT: no ICP problem, a restated thesis (Jaccard ≥ 0.5 against the last 500 ideas), a cited item that is missing, misquoted or unused, an unsupported figure, or a Generalize item's specifics. Rejects are stored as REJECTED with their reasons. The score and priority are computed in code.
  10. Fetch Source Articles → Craft Notes: /api/ideas/craft fetches the article text, and the model describes only its structure. Merge Craft removes any sentence that contains a run of 9 or more words copied from the source.
  11. Assign IDs → Sync Ideas to DB: assigns global sequential IDEA-0001 IDs, upserts every idea with its verdict and flywheel fields, and reports COMPLETED with harvestedCount (kept ideas) and the gate summary.

You decide the intellectual argument and visual metaphor before writing begins.

Reasoning process:
Step 1 — The Hook: For THIS idea, you MUST use the INVERSION hook archetype: Name a common assumption in this field, then what actually happens in practice, in two plain sentences. Thoughtful, never dramatic, and never the "It isn't X, it's Y" formula. Write 1-2 plain sentences in exactly that style, the way the author would say it to a peer: no slogan, no 'it isn't X, it's Y' formula.
Step 2 — The Reader: Describe the most specific real person implied by the ICP, the Brand Context and this idea. Never default to a software or engineering persona unless the brand and idea support it.
Step 2b — The Argument: One central argument, built on the author's advantage when one is given. Every key point must serve it.
Step 3 — The Visual Art Direction for Cover Art:
  - First decide the visual register from the article's ACTUAL subject matter: PHYSICAL_TRADE or ABSTRACT_DATA.
  - Cover Headline: 4 to 8 words and at most 55 characters, in sentence case, so it sets in two or three lines a phone can read. It is the post's title on the thumbnail: someone scrolling past must know at once what the post is about. Name the specific things the post names (the tool, product, process or change) and what happened or why, and make it make sense with no other context. Title it as a specific question or claim about the reader's situation (e.g. 'Why voice agents lose bookings after hours'), never an abstract maxim. Never an abstract category pairing (not 'Fluency Vs Accuracy', 'Speed Versus Quality', or 'AI Scheduling Explained'), never a bare topic label.
  - Emphasis Word: the one word of the cover headline it turns on, usually the thing it names. It is set in the accent colour.
  - Cover Subtitle: One sentence under 90 characters: what the reader gets or what it costs them, in plain editorial-magazine language. NEVER start with 'Explaining', 'Understanding', 'A look at', 'How to', or any other explainer-caption framing. No figure the idea does not state.

Otherwise leave every field empty. Never invent or round a figure.
  - Secondary Visual Motif: One vivid sentence describing a SINGLE small flat line-icon detail that echoes this topic. Never a full studio scene, never multiple objects, never a table or professional-photography setup.
  - Illustration: pick the ONE shape, the ONE figure and the ONE line icon from the collections below whose meaning best matches what this post is about (its subject, not a word it happens to use).
